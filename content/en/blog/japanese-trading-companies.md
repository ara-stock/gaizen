## What Is a Japanese Trading Company (Sogo Shosha)?

A Japanese trading company (sogo shosha) is a diversified conglomerate that **combines trade intermediation with direct equity investment** across resources, commodities, and services — without manufacturing anything itself. The absence of factories keeps fixed costs light and enables flexibility to rotate investments as markets shift.

"Commerce is fundamentally about buying low and selling higher" — sourcing goods, technology, and services and moving them at scale. Japanese trading companies do this globally across resources, food, energy, finance, real estate, and IT.

## Why I Invest Heavily in Trading Companies

### 1. Concessions That Generate Continuous Cash Flow

The core strength of trading companies is **ownership of equity stakes (concessions) in resources, infrastructure, and businesses**. Coal, LNG, copper, grain, power — these concessions generate recurring cash flows every year, providing a highly stable "floor" for the business.

They are cyclically sensitive, but concessions don't evaporate easily. Once secured, they produce revenue for decades.

### 2. Irreplaceable Hubs in Trade Networks

Trading companies are **hubs connecting buyers and sellers**. Their established customer networks, logistics infrastructure, and financial capabilities combine to enable deals that would be impossible for standalone players.

The existence of pre-existing customer relationships is often overlooked, but it's enormously valuable. The ability to bring new opportunities to established counterparties — without paying new customer acquisition costs — is a durable competitive advantage.

### 3. Asset-Light Structure

No owned factories means no heavy fixed-cost burden. Investments can be rotated as times change. Where a manufacturer is locked into a specific business, a trading company can shift from resources to digital when conditions call for it.

This **lightness** is the source of their adaptability.

### 4. Concentration of Top-Tier Talent

Japanese trading companies have consistently attracted top-tier university graduates. The quality of the people making investment decisions, conducting negotiations, and managing risk is a competitive advantage that doesn't appear on balance sheets.

## Strengths and Focus Areas by Company

Each company is called a "sogo shosha" (general trading company), but each has **its own distinct strengths and areas of focus**.

### Mitsubishi Corp (8058) — Balanced Market Leader

Broad coverage from energy (LNG, coal) to metals (copper), food, machinery, and chemicals. Also the largest shareholder of Lawson, giving it deep involvement in domestic consumer infrastructure. Scale and balance are its defining strengths.

### Itochu Corp (8001) — Non-Resource Leader with Outstanding ROE

**Highest non-resource revenue mix** among the five majors — textiles, food, retail, FamilyMart. Relatively stable in resource price downturns. ROE consistently among the highest of the group. Vertical integration down to the consumer end of the supply chain is a distinguishing feature.

### Mitsui & Co (8031) — Resources and New Frontiers

**Among the largest resource concessions** — LNG, iron ore, copper. High exposure to commodity cycles, but exceptional profit leverage in favorable markets. Increasingly active in healthcare and wellness investment.

### Sumitomo Corp (8053) — Urban Development, Real Estate, and Digital

Key profit segments in FY March 2025 include transportation equipment and construction machinery, urban development and real estate, and energy transition. In 2025, Sumitomo completed a TOB (tender offer) of IT services major SCSK for approximately ¥882B, making it a wholly owned subsidiary and central pillar of its digital and AI strategy. The company is increasing its weighting toward real estate development and IT/digital, with a high non-resource ratio supporting stable cash flows.

### Marubeni Corp (8002) — Grain and Power

One of the **world's largest grain trading operations**. The strategic value of food and agriculture grows over the long term. Also operates power projects globally, with renewable energy positioning strengthening its long-term competitive moat.

### Kanematsu (8020) — High-Margin Niche Specialist in ICT

A **specialist trading company** focused on electronics/ICT and food. A large portion of revenue comes from IT systems maintenance contracts — a recurring, subscription-like revenue structure that supports consistently high margins. Much smaller in scale than the five majors; I currently hold a modest test position. But in terms of revenue quality, it merits attention.

## How I Think About Trading Companies as Investments

### Conviction in the Current Business

The first thing I verify is **whether the business is generating appropriate profits, margins, and growth today**.

Trading company earnings have risen substantially in recent years — partly from resource price tailwinds, but also from improvements in non-resource profitability. The quality of earnings has improved. PER in the 10x range looks undervalued relative to the demonstrated ability to generate stable cash flows.

### Expectation for Future Business Expansion

Trading companies' strength lies in **their ability to reinvest profits into new businesses and compound growth**.

- **Resources**: Maintain and expand existing concessions. Coal demand declines with decarbonization, but copper, lithium, and LNG — resources essential to the energy transition — remain in demand
- **Non-resources**: Ongoing accumulation in digital, healthcare, and renewable energy
- **Global markets**: Infrastructure development in emerging economies and growing food demand play to trading companies' strengths

Warren Buffett's well-known investments in Japan's five major trading companies reflected the same structural logic — stable cash flows, disciplined capital allocation, and low valuations.

## Summary

Trading companies are not "jacks of all trades." I see them as **enterprises housing three compounding engines: concessions, trade flow networks, and human capital**.

Each company has distinct specializations with different balances of resource, non-resource, and geographic exposure. My core position is in the five majors, with attention to each company's cyclical risks — a long-term commitment to participating in the backbone of Japan's industrial competitiveness.
