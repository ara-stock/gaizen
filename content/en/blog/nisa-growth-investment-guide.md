## The Verdict: S&P500 or All-Country

If you're not sure what to put in your NISA, **eMAXIS Slim US Equities (S&P500) and eMAXIS Slim All Country are strong options to consider.**

That's the conclusion. The rest of this article explains why.

## What Is the New NISA Growth Investment Allowance?

The new NISA has two types of accounts:

| Account | Annual limit | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Accumulation allowance | ¥1.2M (¥100K/month) | Index fund auto-investing only |
| Growth investment allowance | ¥2.4M | Stocks, ETFs, investment trusts — broad eligibility |

**The growth allowance is twice the accumulation allowance**, and it allows individual stocks and ETFs. Combined, you can invest up to ¥3.6M/year tax-free, with a lifetime limit of ¥18M.

## How Taxation Works — What Is and Isn't Tax-Free

Gains inside a NISA account are tax-free in Japan, but **foreign taxation may still apply** depending on what you hold.

| Investment | Japan tax | Foreign tax |
|---|---|---|
| Japanese stocks (dividends) | Tax-free ✅ | None |
| US stocks / ETFs (dividends) | Tax-free ✅ | US 10% withholding remains ⚠️ |
| No-distribution index funds (eMAXIS Slim, etc.) | Tax-free ✅ | Foreign taxes may still arise inside the fund |

### Japanese Stock Dividends

Inside NISA, Japanese stock dividends are **fully tax-free** — but only if your account is set to receive dividends via the **"proportional shares distribution" (kabushiki-kazu hirei haibun) method**. Other methods (paper dividend receipts, etc.) will result in taxation even inside NISA.

### US Stocks and ETFs

When you hold US stocks or ETFs like VOO inside NISA, Japan's domestic tax is waived, but **the US government's 10% withholding tax on dividends remains**.

In a regular taxable account, you can claim a foreign tax credit to eliminate double taxation. But **the foreign tax credit is not available inside NISA**, so the US 10% becomes a permanent cost with no offset.

### No-Distribution Index Funds (eMAXIS Slim, etc.)

With no-distribution funds like eMAXIS Slim S&P500 or All-Country, dividends are **reinvested internally within the fund**. No dividend payment reaches the investor. No tax event occurs. The entire structure bypasses dividend taxation at the investor level.

## Why Index Funds Make the Most Efficient Use of NISA

This is the key advantage of using no-distribution index funds in your NISA.

### With Individual Stocks or ETFs

```
Receive dividend → Want to reinvest → Use up more NISA allowance
```

When you receive dividends from individual stocks or ETFs and reinvest them, **you consume additional NISA allowance**. The annual limit is fixed, so having dividends quietly erode your available allowance is an often-overlooked cost.

### With No-Distribution Index Funds

```
Fund automatically reinvests internally → No NISA allowance consumed
```

With no-distribution funds like eMAXIS Slim, dividends are automatically reinvested within the fund. **You continue to benefit from compounding without using any additional NISA allowance.**

This gap grows larger over 5, 10, 20 years. There's also a subtler but important point: **even after you've filled your lifetime ¥18M NISA allowance, the fund continues reinvesting internally — still inside your NISA account, still tax-free.** Once the lifetime cap is reached, any reinvestment of dividends from individual stocks would have to go into a taxable account. That compounding gap only widens with time.

## Is "Japanese Stocks Are More Advantageous" Really True?

"Japanese stock dividends are fully tax-free inside NISA, so Japanese stocks are better" — this argument is common, but incomplete.

### Compare Total Return, Not Dividend Yield

To evaluate a high-dividend strategy, you need **total return — capital gains plus dividends reinvested** — not dividend yield in isolation.

Here's a 5-year total return comparison (April 2020 – March 2025) between three Nikkei-listed stocks and the S&P500:

| Stock | Price gain | Cumulative dividends | Total return |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mitsubishi UFJ (8306) | +239% | ¥151 (~+23% on initial price) | **~+262%** |
| Toyota (7203) | +119% | ¥257 (~+15% on initial price) | **~+134%** |
| KDDI (9433) | +54% | ¥635 (~+37% on initial price) | **~+91%** |
| S&P500 (JPY, incl. dividends) | — | — | **~+134%** |

*Assumes NISA account (Japanese stock dividends tax-free). Toyota adjusted for Oct 2021 1:5 stock split. S&P500 based on 5-year annualized return of ~18.5% JPY-denominated.*

Two takeaways:

**1. Mitsubishi UFJ significantly outperformed S&P500** — but this was driven by the specific tailwind of the Bank of Japan's rate normalization. Banks benefit from rate hikes. That environment is not guaranteed to continue.

**2. KDDI had a high dividend yield but significantly underperformed S&P500 in total return** — "high yield = good investment" is false. Capital appreciation must be included in the comparison.

> Don't focus only on the tax benefit. Compare post-tax, dividend-inclusive total return.

### NISA Allowance Efficiency Matters Too

As discussed, reinvesting dividends from individual stocks consumes NISA allowance. Index funds compound internally without consuming it. **For maximizing the value of your NISA, no-distribution index funds win on this dimension as well.**

## How I Use the Growth Investment Allowance

Here's my personal approach:

- **Accumulation allowance (¥100K/month)**: eMAXIS Slim S&P500 or All-Country, auto-invested monthly
- **Growth investment allowance**: Mix of individual stocks (trading companies, etc.) and index funds. Use NISA aggressively for holdings that can deliver both capital appreciation and dividends

Putting the entire growth allowance into a single index fund is also a highly rational, simple strategy. What matters more than which option you choose is **using the allowance consistently without leaving it idle**.

## Summary

- **For the growth allowance: S&P500 or All-Country index fund**
- The tax-free dividend benefit of Japanese stocks is real — but compare **total return including capital gains**
- US stocks and ETFs: 10% US withholding remains. Foreign tax credit is unavailable inside NISA
- No-distribution index funds reinvest internally without consuming NISA allowance — **the most efficient compounding structure for long-term investing**
- Individual stock positions can use the growth allowance, but combining with index funds is practical
