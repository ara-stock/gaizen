## The Name

GAIZEN comes from the Japanese word **蓋然 (gaizen)**, which means probability or likelihood — the degree to which something is likely to be true.

I chose it because it captures how I think about investing. Not prediction. Not timing. Not finding the perfect entry point. Just making consistently high-probability choices and letting time and compounding do the rest.

That's the entire philosophy in one word.

---

## Who Writes This

I'm ara — a salaried employee and investor based in Japan. I run this blog as a way to document how I actually think about money, investing, and building toward financial independence.

My investment style is deliberately unsexy:

- **Core**: Monthly automated accumulation into eMAXIS Slim S&P500 via NISA at Rakuten Securities
- **Satellite**: Individual stocks in sectors I understand — Japanese trading companies, financials, AI infrastructure, select US equities
- **Small positions**: Bitcoin, DeFi yield strategies, gold (via 純金積立)

I'm not aiming for complete early retirement. My goal is **side-FIRE** — gradually shifting from full-time employment toward a mix of investment income and work I choose, rather than work I'm obligated to do.

## Why I Write

There's no shortage of investment content in Japanese. What's harder to find is writing that's honest about the *reasoning* behind specific decisions — not just "here's what index funds are" but "here's why I hold S&P500 instead of All-Country, and here's the personal logic behind that."

Most finance content is either too generic or too promotional. I wanted something in between: detailed enough to be useful, personal enough to be real.

Every article on this blog is written from that frame. When I explain a concept, I try to include where I actually landed on it — not just both sides of an argument.

## Core Investment Principles

**1. Invest in what I understand.**
I don't hold a stock I can't explain in plain language. The business model, the competitive moat, the risk — if I can't state them clearly, I don't invest. This keeps my individual stock portfolio concentrated in sectors I follow closely.

**2. Large companies first.**
Companies with dominant market positions have usually earned them. Brand value, customer lock-in, economies of scale — these tend to compound. The biggest companies are big for a reason.

**3. Core + satellite structure.**
NISA accumulation (index funds) is the engine. Individual stocks and higher-risk positions are layered on top. The core runs on autopilot; the satellite gets active attention.

**4. Long-term means five years minimum.**
Short-term price movements don't change my decisions. What matters is whether the underlying business is growing and whether the competitive position is holding. If yes, I hold.

This blog is not investment advice. I'm not a licensed financial advisor. The articles here document my own reasoning and decisions — they're useful as a reference point, not a prescription.
