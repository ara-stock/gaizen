## Why These Metrics Matter

Picking individual stocks without understanding valuation is like shopping without knowing prices. ROE, PER, and PBR are the most widely used entry-level tools for evaluating whether a company is earning well and whether its stock is priced reasonably.

Understanding them doesn't make stock picking easy — but not understanding them makes it worse.

## ROE (Return on Equity): How Efficiently a Company Earns

### Formula

```
ROE = Net Income ÷ Shareholders' Equity × 100 (%)
```

### What It Measures

How much profit a company generates for each unit of shareholders' equity. An ROE of 15% means the company produces ¥15 of profit for every ¥100 of equity invested.

### Benchmarks

| ROE | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Under 5% | Low — capital being used inefficiently |
| 5–10% | Average |
| 10–20% | Strong |
| Over 20% | Excellent — verify it's sustainable |

**Japanese stocks historically have low ROE.** Tokyo Prime Market averages around 8–10%, compared to 15–20%+ for S&P 500 companies. This structural difference is one reason Japanese equities have lagged US markets over long periods.

The Tokyo Stock Exchange has been actively pressuring listed companies to improve ROE and PBR since 2023, which has led to increased buybacks, dividend hikes, and asset divestitures across Japanese corporates.

### Watch Out For

ROE can be inflated by increasing debt (leverage). A high ROE from a heavily leveraged company is less impressive than the same ROE from a debt-free one. Always check the balance sheet.

## PER (Price-to-Earnings Ratio): Cheap or Expensive Relative to Earnings

### Formula

```
PER = Stock Price ÷ Earnings Per Share (EPS)
```

### What It Measures

"How many years of current earnings am I paying for?" A PER of 20× means the price equals 20 years of current profits. Investors pay more when they expect earnings to grow.

### Benchmarks

| PER | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Under 10× | Potentially cheap (check for problems) |
| 15–20× | Average (varies by sector) |
| 25–30×+ | Premium (growth already priced in) |
| 50×+ | Common for growth/theme stocks |

**PER varies enormously by sector.** A tech company at 30× may be reasonable; a utility company at 30× is expensive. Always compare within the same industry.

**Forward PER (using estimated future earnings) is more useful than trailing PER** for most active decisions. What matters is where earnings are going, not where they've been.

## PBR (Price-to-Book Ratio): Price Relative to Asset Value

### Formula

```
PBR = Stock Price ÷ Book Value Per Share (BPS)
```

### What It Measures

How the market values the company relative to its accounting net assets. PBR of 1× means the stock price equals book value. Below 1× means you can "buy the company for less than its accounting value."

### Benchmarks

| PBR | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Under 1× | Trading below book (value opportunity — or value trap) |
| 1–2× | Standard |
| Over 3× | Market pricing in brand, intangibles, high growth |

**PBR under 1× is not automatically a bargain.** If business prospects are deteriorating or the assets are likely to erode, the discount is justified. The Tokyo Stock Exchange's push for companies to address PBR below 1× has triggered buybacks and restructuring, which can create genuine opportunities — but requires company-by-company analysis.

## Using All Three Together

**Identifying growth stocks:**
- High ROE (15%+) — earning well on equity
- High PER (30×+) — market pricing in future earnings growth
- High PBR (3×+) — brand, network effects, or high-quality assets valued above book

**Identifying value stocks:**
- Low PBR (near 1×) — trading close to book value
- Low PER (under 15×) — stock not fully reflecting earning power
- ROE improving — catalysts visible for future returns

## The Limits: Metrics Are Starting Points, Not Answers

ROE, PER, and PBR are screens — not verdicts.

- **Cross-sector comparison doesn't work.** A bank at PER 10× and a software company at PER 10× are completely different situations
- **They're backward-looking.** Past earnings don't guarantee future results; industry disruption and management decisions aren't captured
- **One-time items distort them.** Special gains, impairment charges, and asset sales can swing net income dramatically in a single year, temporarily breaking PER and ROE

Use these metrics to narrow the field. "This company's ROE is high and PER is reasonable" gives you a reason to look harder — not a reason to buy. The real work is understanding the business, the competitive position, and the management quality that produced those numbers.

## Summary

- **ROE**: Profitability of shareholders' capital. Over 10% is strong; Japan average is 8–10%, US average is 15–20%
- **PER**: Price relative to earnings. Compare within the same sector; forward PER is more useful than trailing
- **PBR**: Price relative to book value. Below 1× can be an opportunity or a warning sign — verify the cause
- Combine all three: high ROE + high PER + high PBR = growth stock profile; low PBR + low PER + improving ROE = value stock profile
- Metrics are filters for deeper research, not conclusions in themselves
