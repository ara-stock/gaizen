## What's the Difference?

ETFs and mutual funds (investment trusts in Japan) both let you invest in a basket of securities. The mechanics are different in ways that matter for practical use.

**Short answer: For NISA recurring investments in Japan, Japanese-domiciled index funds (like eMAXIS Slim S&P500) are the simpler, more tax-efficient choice.** ETFs have their place, but for most investors, mutual funds are where to start.

## Feature Comparison

| Feature | ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) | Mutual Fund (Investment Trust) |
|---|---|---|
| Trading | Real-time on stock exchange | Once daily at NAV |
| Minimum investment | 1 unit (varies by product) | ¥100+ |
| Purchase fee | Per trade (increasingly free) | Usually none (no-load) |
| Expense ratio | Very low (0.03–0.20%) | Low-cost funds ~0.09% |
| Dividend reinvestment | Usually manual | Automatic in NISA |
| Foreign currency needed | Yes (for US-listed ETFs) | No (yen-denominated) |
| Credit card investment | Not supported | Supported (Rakuten, SBI) |

## Why Mutual Funds Win for Most Investors

### Start with ¥100, automate everything

Mutual funds accept recurring investments from as little as ¥100 per month. Set it up once, and the contribution, purchase, and reinvestment happen automatically. NISA recurring investment quota allows up to ¥1.2M per year.

### Dividends reinvest automatically — and tax-free in NISA

eMAXIS Slim funds don't distribute dividends at all. Everything compounds inside the fund. Inside a NISA account, there are no taxable events until you sell. This is compounding working at maximum efficiency.

### Credit card points on every contribution

Rakuten Securities (Rakuten Card) and SBI Securities (Mitsui Sumitomo Card) let you pay for recurring mutual fund investments with a credit card and earn points — up to 0.5–5% depending on the card. Those points add up meaningfully over years. ETFs don't qualify for this.

## Where ETFs Have the Edge

### Marginally lower expense ratios

US ETFs reach expenses that mutual funds can't quite match: VTI at 0.03%, VOO at 0.03%. eMAXIS Slim S&P500 at 0.09% is close — but on a ¥30M portfolio, the gap is ~¥18,000/year. Small but real.

### Real-time trading and limit orders

ETFs trade like stocks during market hours. If you want to buy at a specific price or react quickly to market moves, ETFs give you that control. Mutual funds execute at the end-of-day price regardless of when you submit the order.

### Products only available as US ETFs

VYM, SPYD, HDV, QQQ, and many sector and thematic ETFs have no Japanese mutual fund equivalent. If you want them, you buy the ETF.

## The Double-Taxation Problem with US ETFs in NISA

This is the key reason US ETFs are less efficient than Japanese mutual funds inside NISA.

When a US-listed ETF pays dividends, the US withholds 10% tax at source. In a standard taxable account (特定口座), you can reclaim this via foreign tax credit (外国税額控除) on your tax return. But **inside a NISA account, foreign tax credits don't apply** — that 10% is gone.

Japanese mutual funds like eMAXIS Slim are domiciled in Japan. They handle any internal foreign withholding within the fund structure, and your NISA account sees no taxable dividends at all.

**Result: Japanese index funds are more tax-efficient than US ETFs inside NISA.**

## Which to Use: A Practical Framework

**NISA Recurring Investment Quota → Mutual Fund**

eMAXIS Slim S&P500 or eMAXIS Slim All Country. Automatic investment, credit card points, yen-denominated, no currency conversion, maximum tax efficiency in NISA. No reason to complicate this.

**NISA Growth Investment Quota or Taxable Account → ETFs as needed**

- VYM / HDV / SPYD (US high-dividend ETFs)
- QQQ (NASDAQ 100)
- VT (global equities)
- SPY / VOO (S&P 500 — if the slightly lower expense ratio matters to you)

Use ETFs to access products that have no Japanese mutual fund equivalent, or when you want to trade at specific prices.

## Summary

- **Mutual funds**: Easier to automate, credit card points, tax-efficient in NISA, yen-denominated. The right starting point for almost everyone
- **ETFs**: Lower expense ratios, real-time trading, access to products unavailable as Japanese funds. Best used with specific intent
- NISA recurring quota → eMAXIS Slim index fund, no question
- Growth quota and taxable accounts → add ETFs where they give you something the fund equivalent can't
