## Bottom Line First: If You're Targeting Early Retirement Young, iDeCo Can Wait

Let me share my priority order upfront.

**NISA (tax-exempt investment account) → 特定口座 (taxable brokerage account) → Savings → iDeCo**

iDeCo comes last. This is not me saying "iDeCo is a bad deal." It's just that the widely repeated sales pitch of "amazing tax savings" is missing some important footnotes. I want you to understand those footnotes clearly before making your decision.

---

## What Is a Defined Contribution Pension?

A Defined Contribution pension (DC) is a retirement savings scheme where you make contributions yourself, and the amount you ultimately receive depends on your investment results. There are two main types:

| | 企業型DC (Corporate DC) | iDeCo (Individual-type Defined Contribution pension) |
|---|---|---|
| Who can join | Employees whose company has adopted the scheme | In principle, National Pension enrollees aged 20–65 |
| Who contributes | Primarily the company (personal contributions also possible) | The individual |
| Monthly contribution limit (for company employees) | In principle ¥55,000 (if no other corporate pension) | ¥23,000 (if no Corporate DC or defined-benefit pension) / ¥20,000 when combined with Corporate DC |
| Investment decisions | The individual | The individual |
| When you can withdraw | In principle, from age 60 onward | In principle, from age 60 onward |

I am myself subject to a Corporate DC plan, so I understand the conditions for combining it with iDeCo from personal experience.

## "Tax Savings" vs. "Tax Deferral"

iDeCo's biggest selling point is that "contributions are fully deductible from taxable income." It's true that your income tax and resident tax in the year you contribute go down.

However, **you are taxed when you withdraw.**

- **If you receive it as a lump sum**: taxed as retirement income
- **If you receive it as a pension (annuity)**: taxed every year as miscellaneous income

In other words, the difference is simply whether you pay now or later — it's "tax deferral."

> iDeCo is not tax-exempt; it's a mechanism that shifts the timing of taxation into the future. The term "tax savings" is, strictly speaking, not accurate.

---

## The "Escape Hatch" of the 退職所得控除 (Retirement Income Deduction)

That said, if you plan your withdrawal method carefully, you may be able to bring the effective tax rate down significantly. The key is the **退職所得控除 (retirement income deduction)**.

When you take the money as a lump sum, the retirement income deduction applies. The deduction amount grows with the number of years you have participated, and **for more than 20 years of participation, it is ¥700,000 per year**. For example, with 30 years of participation, the estimated deduction is reportedly over ¥15 million (exact figures vary depending on years of service and other conditions).

By leveraging this mechanism, long-term participants may find their effective tax rate at withdrawal is very low. This is a case where "deferral" works in your favor.

However, this only applies if you are **receiving the money at age 60 or later**.

---

## Why the Age-60 Lock-In Is a Fatal Flaw for Early Retirement Seekers

This is the core problem.

Money contributed to iDeCo **cannot, in principle, be withdrawn until you turn 60** (the 2022 reform allows you to defer receiving it up to age 75 depending on participation period, but the conditions for early withdrawal remain unchanged).

If you are aiming for early retirement in your 40s or 50s, the balance in your iDeCo is **not money you can freely use as an asset.**

Say you are trying to FIRE at 45 and you have ¥5 million in iDeCo — that money is "frozen" for 15-plus years. You cannot use it for living expenses. Counting it in your FIRE asset calculation is dangerous.

**NISA and 特定口座 (taxable brokerage account) assets can be sold at any time. iDeCo cannot. This difference in liquidity is decisive for anyone aiming for early retirement.**

## On Fees

One thing that tends to get overlooked is fees. iDeCo involves several layers of charges:

| Fee Type | Details |
|---|---|
| Enrollment / transfer fee | Paid to the National Pension Fund Association (approx. ¥2,829) |
| Account maintenance fee | ¥171/month (National Pension Fund Association + trust bank portion) |
| Record-keeping institution fee | Varies by financial institution (¥0 to several hundred yen/month) |

Depending on the financial institution, fees can exceed several hundred yen per month. Annual costs can reach several thousand yen to over ¥10,000.

Once you **start iDeCo, fees keep accumulating even if you stop contributing** (basic fees continue while you maintain the account as an investment-only participant). The design makes it difficult to simply stop contributions and leave the account dormant.

## My Priority Order: NISA → Taxable Account → Savings → iDeCo

Let me lay this out again.

**1. NISA (新NISA — New NISA) first**
Up to ¥3.6 million per year, ¥18 million lifetime, with investment gains completely tax-exempt. Can be sold at any time. Liquid. This is the first bucket anyone targeting early retirement should fill.

**2. 特定口座 (taxable brokerage account)**
After using up your NISA allowance, you build up assets in a taxable account. Capital gains and dividends are subject to roughly 20% tax, but you retain the freedom to liquidate at any time. A rational approach for growing wealth while preserving liquidity.

**3. Savings (Cash)**
In principle, having roughly one month of living expenses in cash is enough. If you want a thicker safety cushion for emergencies, prioritize securing about six months' worth of cash before considering iDeCo. Liquidity is the top priority here.

**4. iDeCo**
After the above three are in order, assess your situation and make a judgment based on what's left over.

## Who Should Actually Consider It

I'm not saying iDeCo is unnecessary. It is a strong option for the following types of people.

**People in their 50s or older with less than 10 years until age 60**
The shorter the remaining lock-in period, the lower the liquidity risk. These are conditions where it is easier to take advantage of the retirement income deduction.

**Self-employed individuals, freelancers, and business owners**
They may have more tax-reduction tools available compared to company employees, but they can use iDeCo with a higher monthly contribution limit (¥68,000) unavailable to salaried workers. The higher your taxable income, the greater the effect of the income deduction. Worth considering as an option after you have exhausted other tax reduction tools (小規模企業共済 — small enterprise mutual aid, expense deductions, etc.).

**Salaried employees without Corporate DC who have maxed out their NISA allowance**
A candidate for those who have filled their lifetime NISA allowance (¥18 million) and want to add more tax-advantaged long-term investment capacity.

## Combining Corporate DC and iDeCo

Due to the rule change in October 2022, employees enrolled in a Corporate DC can now simultaneously enroll in iDeCo. However, **there are several conditions.**

- Your employer's plan rules must allow for combined enrollment with iDeCo (in some cases, the plan rules must be amended)
- The combined total of the employer's Corporate DC contributions and your iDeCo contributions must not exceed a certain ceiling

If you are currently enrolled in a Corporate DC, the first step is to confirm the details of your company's plan.

---

## Summary

- iDeCo is not "tax savings" — it is **"tax deferral."** You are taxed at the point of withdrawal too
- By leveraging the 退職所得控除 (retirement income deduction), there is a possibility of keeping the effective tax rate low
- **The lock-in until age 60 is the single biggest obstacle for early retirement seekers**
- Fees are small but they accumulate, and once you start it is hard to stop
- My priority order: **NISA → taxable account → savings → iDeCo**
- **If you're aiming for FIRE while young, put iDeCo at the bottom of the list. Those in their 50s or older, the self-employed, and those who have maxed out NISA are worth reconsidering**
