## Good News First: Most Index Investors Don't Need to File

If you're investing in Japanese-domiciled index funds (like eMAXIS Slim S&P500) inside a NISA account, you don't need to file a tax return. NISA gains, dividends, and sale proceeds are all tax-exempt. There's nothing to report.

This is one of the biggest practical advantages of starting with NISA and Japanese index funds.

## How Tokutei Accounts Work (特定口座, Withholding Type)

For investments held outside NISA — in a 特定口座 with withholding (源泉徴収あり) — your brokerage handles everything automatically.

- Capital gains from sales: 20.315% withheld automatically
- Dividends and distributions: 20.315% withheld automatically

With a single brokerage in this account type, you have no filing obligation. The brokerage takes care of it.

## When Filing Is Worth It

### 1. Loss offsetting across multiple accounts (損益通算)

If you have gains at Brokerage A and losses at Brokerage B in the same year, you can combine them in a tax return. If your gain is ¥500,000 and your loss is ¥300,000, you only pay tax on ¥200,000 — and the excess tax already withheld gets refunded.

This only applies when you have accounts at multiple brokerages. Single-brokerage investors don't need to bother.

### 2. Carrying forward losses (繰越控除)

If you close a year with a net loss, filing a tax return lets you carry that loss forward for up to three years. Future gains get offset against it, reducing your tax bill.

If you don't file, the loss is gone permanently.

### 3. Foreign tax credit on US ETF dividends (外国税額控除)

US-listed ETFs (VYM, QQQ, VOO, etc.) have US withholding tax — 10% — applied to dividends before they reach you. In a taxable 特定口座, you can reclaim this by claiming a foreign tax credit on your return.

**Important caveat: the foreign tax credit does not apply to US ETFs held inside a NISA account.** NISA eliminates Japanese domestic taxes, not foreign withholding. That 10% stays gone regardless of where you hold the ETF inside NISA.

This is why Japanese index funds are more tax-efficient in NISA than US ETFs — there's no foreign withholding to worry about.

### 4. Low-income year gains

Salaried employees with investment gains under ¥200,000/year generally don't need to file (though local resident tax may still apply). If you have losses above that threshold, filing to carry them forward is usually worthwhile.

## How to Claim Foreign Tax Credit (Brief Overview)

1. Get the annual trading statement (年間取引報告書) from your brokerage
2. Find the "Foreign Income Tax Amount" line — this is the US withholding paid on ETF dividends
3. Attach "Foreign Tax Credit Statement" (外国税額控除に関する明細書) to your return
4. The credit equals the lower of: the foreign tax paid, or the credit limit calculated from your Japanese tax liability

e-Tax (Japan's online filing system) handles this entirely online. It's more straightforward than it sounds.

## Account Type Tax Comparison

| Account | Capital Gains Tax | Dividend Tax | Foreign Tax Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| NISA (all quotas) | Tax-exempt | Tax-exempt (domestic) | Not applicable |
| 特定口座 (withholding) | 20.315% auto-withheld | 20.315% auto-withheld | Available by filing |
| General account | 20.315% (file yourself) | Same | Available |

NISA is powerful but doesn't help with foreign withholding. For US ETFs, a taxable 特定口座 with an annual tax return is actually more tax-efficient than NISA on the dividend portion.

## Quick Decision Checklist

✅ NISA only, Japanese mutual funds only → **No filing needed**

✅ Single 特定口座 (withholding) brokerage, no losses → **No filing needed** (optional to optimize)

✅ Gains at one broker, losses at another → **File to offset and get a refund**

✅ US ETF dividends in taxable 特定口座 → **File to reclaim foreign withholding**

✅ Year-end with net losses → **File to carry losses forward for up to 3 years**

## Summary

- NISA + Japanese index funds = no tax return needed, ever
- 特定口座 (withholding type) handles taxes automatically for single-brokerage investors
- Multi-brokerage loss offsetting, foreign tax credits on US ETF dividends, and loss carryforwards are the main reasons to file
- US ETFs in NISA still pay 10% US withholding — Japanese index funds don't have this issue
- e-Tax makes online filing accessible — it's worth learning if you hold US ETFs in taxable accounts
