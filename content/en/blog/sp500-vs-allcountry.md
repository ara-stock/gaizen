## The Verdict: Either Works

**For long-term accumulation, S&P500 and All-Country (eMAXIS Slim All Country) are not meaningfully different.**

If you can't decide and that paralysis is keeping you out of the market, split it 50/50 and start today. The time you spend deliberating has a greater negative impact on long-term returns than any difference between these two funds.

## What Is the S&P500?

The S&P500 is a stock index covering the major US-listed companies — not exactly 500 companies, but **companies that meet strict criteria for market cap, liquidity, and profitability**. The current count is around 503 stocks.

Weights are determined by **market-cap weighting** — larger companies have larger index weights. Apple, Microsoft, and NVIDIA hold the top positions, automatically reflecting market participants' collective assessment.

## What Is All-Country (Orukan)?

eMAXIS Slim All Country tracks the **MSCI All Country World Index**, covering approximately 3,000 stocks across 23 developed markets and 24 emerging markets — 47 countries total — also market-cap weighted.

### All-Country Is Still ~63% US Stocks

Despite the "global" framing, **the US accounts for approximately 63% of All-Country's weight**. The top holdings — Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA — are the same as in S&P500. "Buying All-Country avoids overlap with S&P500" is a misconception; both are essentially US large-cap portfolios.

## Using Both in NISA

The new NISA has two types of accounts:

- **Accumulation allowance**: ¥1.2M/year (¥100K/month). The place to start. Designed for regular, long-term investing.
- **Growth investment allowance**: ¥2.4M/year. For individual stocks, ETFs, and additional index funds.

I max out the accumulation allowance monthly and use the growth allowance for individual stocks and additional index funds. Putting the entire growth allowance into a single index fund is also a clean, highly rational strategy.

Putting ¥100K/month into S&P500 or All-Country and letting compounding run for years is powerful. Use your allowance fully, consistently.

## What Matters Most Is Starting

### Buy Both If You Can't Decide

"I can't choose" is the worst state to be in. Buying half of each means you're in the market. Adjust as you learn what suits you.

### Earlier Is Better

Time is your most powerful investment tool. Starting early maximizes the compounding window.

Late starters have less time to recover from drawdowns. And without experience with market volatility, a sharp decline can feel devastating, increasing the risk of selling at exactly the wrong moment. Experiencing the ups and downs with small amounts early on builds the resilience needed for larger positions later.

### Crashes Can Happen — And Recovery Time Is Uncertain

When COVID emptied the streets, when tariff policy sent markets sharply lower — both events resolved within months to a few years.

> "Almost no one can consistently time the market to enter at the bottom and exit at the top."

Even if you sell successfully during a crash, identifying the bottom is nearly impossible — fear prevents re-entry. And the counterpart: buying when "it should keep going up" often means catching the top. Trying to time the market is the single greatest destroyer of long-term returns.

**For me, the priority is a system that makes consistent accumulation easy to sustain.**

## Strategy After You Start: Keep Buying

### Auto-Invest the Same Amount Every Month

After you start, the strategy is simple: **buy a fixed amount automatically every month. Set it once and forget it.**

The moment you start adjusting — "let me buy more this month" or "I'll sit this one out" — you're entering the market-timing trap. Human emotions move inversely to price: confident when markets are up, scared when they're down. That instinct destroys returns.

### Down Markets Are Cheap Markets

Monthly DCA buys more units when prices are low and fewer when prices are high, which can smooth the average purchase cost. If income and emergency savings remain secure, continuing a pre-planned contribution through a decline is one option. If household finances worsen, adjust the contribution first.

### Stop Checking So Often

You don't need to check your NAV every day. The more frequently you watch, the more your emotions respond to short-term noise, and the more likely you are to make poor decisions.

Once a month, verify that your auto-invest is running. That's enough. The discipline of **letting nothing happen** is the strategy.

### Being Bored Is the Goal

Index accumulation is genuinely boring. Nothing to do. That's the point.

Let the market work. Use the time and mental energy you'd otherwise spend on investing — on work, family, hobbies, health. Freedom from obsessing over markets is a real, non-financial return from this approach.

### Don't Think About When to Sell

Once you start thinking about when to sell, you're also thinking about when to time the market. That leads back to the timing trap.

An exit strategy becomes relevant only when approaching FIRE or a major life event. Until then: don't sell. Just accumulate. **Continued contributions contribute more to long-term returns than any exit timing decision.**

## Summary

- **S&P500 vs. All-Country: the difference is small. Both are excellent choices**
- **If you can't decide, buy both and start now**
- **Max out the NISA accumulation allowance**
- **Buy the same amount every month, automatically**
- **Don't try to time the market. Don't miss opportunities**

What matters in investing is not picking the perfect fund — it's staying invested for a long time.
