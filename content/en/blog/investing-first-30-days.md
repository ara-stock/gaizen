## The 30-Day Goal Is Not to Learn Everything

You do not need to understand every part of investing before you begin.

The goal for your first 30 days is to complete five tasks:

1. Open a Rakuten Securities account
2. Open Rakuten Bank and connect it to Rakuten Securities
3. Open a NISA account at Rakuten Securities
4. Select an S&P500 index fund
5. Set up a ¥1,000 monthly contribution through the NISA accumulation allowance

You do not need a large portfolio after 30 days. If an automated monthly investment has started running, the first stage is complete.

:::comment
I first invested during my first year of graduate school. One of my classmates was an investor and recommended "A Random Walk Down Wall Street."

I did not read it at the time. Instead, I bought an individual stock after briefly checking its shareholder benefit and dividend yield. I did not understand the business or know what other companies were available. After holding it for several years, I sold when its price had fallen by half.

Much later, I read the book, understood what it meant, and became serious about investing. Looking back, starting small while learning would have been far better than making a large decision before I had a framework.
:::

## Why Start With an Index Fund Instead of an Individual Stock?

Buying an individual stock requires several judgments:

- How the company earns money
- What advantage it has over competitors
- Whether its valuation is high or low relative to earnings
- Whether you would continue holding after business performance deteriorates

New investors often select a stock without knowing the alternatives. Shareholder benefits and dividend yield are only part of the picture; a falling share price or dividend cut can outweigh them.

An index fund tracking the S&P500 spreads your investment across many companies. It does not guarantee your principal, but it reduces the risk of starting with one poorly understood company.

There is no need to rush into individual stocks before your automated index contribution is running and you have a framework for comparing businesses.

## Week 1: Find ¥1,000 You Can Invest

Start with your household finances, not a stock screen.

Review this month's expenses, upcoming payments, and available cash. Decide how much could disappear without affecting your daily life. For this roadmap, the starting amount is **¥1,000 per month**.

¥1,000 will not build wealth quickly. It will, however, let you experience the following at low risk:

- Logging into a brokerage account
- Watching an investment fund move in value
- Seeing a recurring purchase execute
- Getting used to the balance falling below your contribution
- Changing the monthly amount

When I restarted investing seriously in my first year of full-time work, I had approximately ¥500,000 in emergency savings. The appropriate cash reserve varies by employment, household, and monthly expenses. Do not invest money you expect to need soon.

See "[Where to Keep Your Emergency Fund and How Much You Really Need](/en/blog/emergency-fund/)" for a detailed framework.

## Week 2: Apply for Rakuten Securities, Rakuten Bank, and NISA

### Why Rakuten Securities?

SBI Securities was the first brokerage account I opened because my classmate recommended it for IPO applications. Today, I use both SBI Securities and Rakuten Securities.

For a beginner setting up a first recurring investment, I recommend comparing Rakuten Securities first. Its app and account screens are approachable, and Rakuten Bank and Rakuten Card can be connected through one ecosystem.

See "[Rakuten Securities vs SBI Securities](/en/blog/rakuten-vs-sbi-securities/)" for the full comparison.

### Accounts and Settings to Prepare

As you open Rakuten Securities, confirm the following:

| Item | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Rakuten Bank | Simplify transfers between cash and investments |
| Money Bridge | Connect Rakuten Bank and Rakuten Securities |
| NISA account | Make eligible investment gains tax-free |
| Rakuten Card contribution | Automate the monthly fund purchase |

You can use NISA through only one financial institution at a time. If you already use NISA elsewhere, changing financial institutions requires a separate procedure.

Identity verification and tax-office confirmation for NISA can take time. The 30-day roadmap allows room for that waiting period.

## Week 3: Select One Fund and Set ¥1,000 per Month

Once your NISA account is available, select one fund through the accumulation allowance.

I invest in **eMAXIS Slim US Equities (S&P500)**. It aims to track the S&P500 and provides exposure to major US companies in one fund.

S&P500 is not the only valid option. eMAXIS Slim All Country, which spreads investments across global markets, is another candidate. See "[Why I Hold Both S&P500 and All-Country Funds](/en/blog/why-sp500-over-allcountry/)" for the differences and my own decision process.

A simple first setup looks like this:

```
Account: NISA accumulation allowance
Fund: eMAXIS Slim US Equities (S&P500)
Amount: ¥1,000 per month
Payment: Rakuten Card, if available
Distribution option: Reinvestment
```

Several funds have similar names. Before confirming, check the fund name, asset manager, tracked index, and expense ratio.

## Week 4: Confirm the Purchase, Then Leave It Alone

After completing the setup, confirm the scheduled date and purchase result.

Check four things:

- The purchase used the NISA accumulation allowance
- The amount is ¥1,000 per month
- The intended fund was purchased
- Automatic contributions remain scheduled for future months

Once confirmed, there is no need to trade based on daily price movements. Watching ¥1,000 become ¥990 or ¥1,010 helps establish that investment values fluctuate.

The goal of the first month is not profit. It is confirming that the correct account, fund, amount, and schedule are in place.

## What Not to Do During the First 30 Days

### Do Not Buy an Individual Stock on Impulse

I cannot disclose the first stock I bought, but the mistake was buying without understanding the company. Shareholder benefits and a high dividend yield are not enough.

Before selecting individual stocks, review the decision framework in [GAIZEN FINANCE's investment policy](/en/about/), then start small.

### Do Not Force a Large Contribution

You may see claims that everyone should maximize NISA immediately. You do not need to start at ¥100,000 per month. Prioritize living costs and upcoming expenses, then choose an amount you can sustain.

### Do Not Trade Every Day

An easy-to-use investment app does not mean you should trade frequently. At the beginning, checking once a month that the contribution executed correctly is enough.

## Day 31 and Beyond: Increase From ¥1,000

After confirming that the automated contribution works and your household finances remain comfortable, increase the amount gradually.

```
¥1,000/month
  ↓ Learn the setup and experience price movement
¥5,000/month
  ↓ Confirm the effect on your household budget
¥10,000+/month
  ↓ Adjust to income, expenses, and goals
```

Base increases on available monthly cash, not on whether the market recently rose or fell.

I currently use Rakuten Card contributions in my Rakuten Securities NISA account to invest automatically in eMAXIS Slim US Equities (S&P500). I did not begin at my current contribution amount. I increased it after understanding the system and confirming that I could continue.

## Summary

- Use the first 30 days to start an automated contribution, not master every investment topic
- Beginners can start with a diversified index fund instead of rushing into individual stocks
- Connecting Rakuten Securities, Rakuten Bank, NISA, and card contributions can simplify automation
- Begin with ¥1,000 to learn the setup and experience price movement
- After day 30, increase based on household cash flow rather than market direction

:::comment
The main cause of my first investment loss was not the amount invested. It was choosing an individual stock before I understood what I was buying.

A ¥1,000 contribution will not create large short-term profits. But it takes you through opening an account, choosing a fund, experiencing price movement, and sustaining an automated contribution. That is enough to turn investing from something you plan to study someday into something that continues every month.
