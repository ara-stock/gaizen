## My conclusion: advertising funds the next layer of AI infrastructure

I own Alphabet because it combines major user touchpoints, including YouTube and Google Search, with Google Cloud, data centers, custom TPUs, and a global network inside the same corporate group.

I do not see Alphabet as only an advertising company or simply an AI growth stock. I see it as a company that can use cash generated by advertising to build its next growth platform in AI and cloud computing. Heavy AI spending alone does not validate the thesis, however. Cloud revenue and profit must show that the investment can earn a return.

| Decision factor | My current view |
|---|---|
| Original reason to buy | YouTube's durable user reach and advertising platform |
| Core thesis today | Growth in Google Cloud and AI, supported by owned infrastructure |
| Trigger for a new review | A decline of 5% or more, followed by a fresh business and valuation review |
| Intended holding period | At least five years, with no planned exit date |
| Thesis review condition | Advertising and Cloud profit growth stalls across several quarters |

This table describes my own process, not a forecast or guarantee of returns.

---

## YouTube was my original reason to buy

My starting point was not Gemini. It was YouTube. Creators continually produce new videos to earn viewers' attention. Alphabet does not need to make all of that content itself: competition among creators refreshes the platform and helps preserve its relationship with users.

As long as viewers continue to gather there, YouTube remains valuable to advertisers. YouTube advertising revenue reached $11.055 billion in the quarter ended June 2026, up roughly 13% from $9.796 billion a year earlier. The advertising platform that first attracted me was still growing in the latest reported quarter.

:::comment
My main reason for first buying Alphabet was its ownership of YouTube as advertising infrastructure. When I assess a company, I care about who controls the relationship with users.

Creators compete to make content that viewers choose to watch, which continually refreshes the platform. I saw that self-reinforcing cycle as one of Alphabet's most valuable assets.
:::

Long-term relevance does not guarantee that advertising revenue will grow at the same pace. I therefore separate viewing demand from ad revenue, subscription growth, and content acquisition costs.

## My main focus today is Google Cloud and AI

YouTube drove my initial purchase, but Google Cloud and AI now sit at the center of the thesis. Alphabet owns not only the Gemini model but also data centers, servers, networking, and custom-designed TPUs.

Google Cloud describes TPUs as purpose-built AI accelerators that are also available to external customers through its cloud platform. Google's global network spans more than 200 countries and territories and includes a large terrestrial and subsea fiber footprint. Alphabet does not build every component alone; it also develops cables with telecom partners and leases fiber. Even so, its ability to design and operate a broad portion of the compute and network stack matters.

### Growth and investment in the latest quarter

| Metric | Q2 2025 | Q2 2026 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Alphabet revenue | $96.428B | $119.796B | +24% |
| YouTube advertising revenue | $9.796B | $11.055B | about +13% |
| Google Cloud revenue | $13.624B | $24.768B | about +82% |
| Google Cloud operating income | $2.826B | $8.814B | about 3.1x |

Google Cloud's operating margin was roughly 36% on a simple calculation. At the same time, capital expenditure reached $80.598 billion in the first half of 2026, more than double the $39.643 billion spent a year earlier. Alphabet also began recognizing revenue from TPU system sales in Q2 2026, so not all of Cloud's growth should automatically be treated as recurring cloud consumption.

That leaves three questions to track together:

1. Is Google Cloud revenue growth continuing?
2. Can operating profit rise while absorbing technical infrastructure costs?
3. Are operating cash flow and future earnings keeping pace with capital expenditure?

:::comment
I expect AI demand to keep growing. In that market, controlling infrastructure matters because a provider that owns more of its compute stack has more room to improve performance and cost.

Ownership alone does not guarantee lower cost, however. If capacity is built ahead of demand, fixed costs remain. I now focus less on the fact that Alphabet owns infrastructure and more on whether it can convert that infrastructure into profit.
:::

## A 5% decline is a review trigger, not an automatic buy

I rarely reconsider the position after a 1% or 2% move. That is normal daily volatility and says little about business value. A decline of 5% or more is large enough to prompt a new review.

The decline itself does not establish undervaluation. A stock can fall 5% while expected earnings fall 10%. My process is:

1. Identify whether the move came from the market, earnings, regulation, or competition.
2. Check for structural changes in advertising and Cloud revenue or profit.
3. Compare valuation with Alphabet's own history and other holdings under consideration.
4. Decide whether the expected growth is more attractive than other uses of capital.
5. Check whether an additional purchase would make the position too concentrated.

:::comment
I compare expected growth and current valuation across the companies I own. I do not buy Alphabet only because it is cheaper than it was yesterday.

A decline of more than 5% is a reason to investigate. I add only when the thesis is intact and Alphabet remains more attractive than the alternatives.
:::

## I do not trade after every earnings report

I do not buy or sell immediately after every result, and most quarters lead to no action. I care more about a change in business direction than a short-term difference from market expectations.

Still, owning an individual stock requires periodic review of official results. Trading and reviewing are separate decisions. I look more closely when:

- the share price moves sharply;
- the quality of a product I use, such as Gemini, changes materially;
- Google Cloud pricing or supply conditions change significantly; or
- capital expenditure rises without corresponding Cloud growth and profit.

Product experience can reveal a change before it becomes obvious in financial statements, but one user's experience is only one observation. I verify it against official revenue, profit, and demand indicators before making an investment decision.

## What would invalidate the thesis?

My original answer was that the thesis would fail if advertising or Cloud profits stalled or turned negative. A single weak quarter is too crude a test, however. Growth spending and one-time costs can temporarily reduce margins.

I now look for several of the following conditions persisting across multiple quarters:

- Search and YouTube usage and advertising revenue weaken without a credible path to recovery;
- Google Cloud growth and operating margin deteriorate together;
- heavy AI capital expenditure fails to produce corresponding revenue, profit, and cash flow;
- Gemini loses product competitiveness and Alphabet cannot distribute AI through its existing user touchpoints; or
- regulation or litigation materially restricts advertising distribution or default service placement.

One item alone would not force an immediate sale. I would ask why it happened, whether it can be corrected, and whether the expected return remains competitive with other investments.

---

## A lifetime holding still needs an exit framework

I invest with a minimum five-year horizon and, ideally, no planned sale date. Large companies reach their scale through demand, management, talent, brands, and physical assets. Data centers and global networks require capital and time and cannot be replicated overnight.

Scale is not a guarantee of permanence. A large company can miss a technology shift, face a structural regulatory change, or invest inefficiently. Holding for life means refusing to react to every quarterly price move, not refusing to define when the original thesis has failed.

:::comment
I generally invest as though I will hold a company for life, with five years as a minimum horizon. I ask whether demand for the business can persist across that period.

But size alone is not my source of confidence. If the connection between YouTube's reach and profitable Cloud and AI growth breaks, I will revisit the long-term holding assumption.
:::

## Summary

The Alphabet cycle I am investing in is straightforward:

**Search and YouTube attract users and advertisers; advertising funds Cloud and AI; owned infrastructure is converted into products and earnings.**

In Q2 2026, YouTube advertising and Google Cloud both grew, and Cloud profit expanded. Capital expenditure also accelerated sharply. I will judge the thesis by profit and cash flow generated from that investment, not by the strength of the AI narrative alone.

This article is not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Individual stocks can lose value, and the criteria above are a record of my personal decision process.
