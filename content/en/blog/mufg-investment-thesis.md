## My conclusion: this is not a one-variable bet on rates

I first bought Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group (MUFG) against a backdrop of a weaker yen, rising domestic rates, and the launch of the new NISA. My view has since become more precise. I now assess MUFG as a diversified financial group combining domestic lending, overseas businesses, asset management, and fee income.

Bank stocks are often reduced to the claim that higher rates mean higher profits. In practice, deposit rates, loan demand, securities holdings, and credit costs move at the same time. The policy rate alone cannot determine whether I should add or sell.

| Decision factor | My current view |
|---|---|
| Original reasons to buy | A weaker yen, rising rates, and new-NISA business opportunities |
| Core thesis today | Better domestic lending spreads plus diversified overseas and fee businesses |
| Guideline for adding | Business growth intact, rates stable to rising, and forward dividend yield above 3% |
| Intended holding period | At least five years, with no planned exit date |
| Thesis review condition | Combined deterioration in spreads, credit costs, and capital, not merely one rate cut |

This table describes my personal process. It is not a forecast or guarantee of dividends or returns.

## My three original reasons to buy

### 1. A weaker yen and overseas earnings

I expected the yen to remain weak and wanted exposure to companies that held or earned foreign currency. MUFG has a broad overseas earnings base through international branches, Krungsri in Thailand, Bank Danamon in Indonesia, and its equity-method stake in Morgan Stanley.

However, **owning MUFG shares is not the same as owning US dollars**. A weaker yen can increase the translated value of foreign earnings, but hedging, foreign-currency funding costs, and local credit costs also matter. MUFG is a yen-denominated equity with foreign-currency sensitivity, not a substitute for a dollar deposit or dollar-denominated asset.

### 2. Higher domestic interest rates

Higher domestic rates can widen the difference between loan and deposit rates and increase net interest income. MUFG reported consolidated net interest income of ¥882.3 billion in Q1 FY2026, up ¥191.6 billion year over year. The company estimated that higher yen interest rates contributed approximately ¥60 billion to operating profit.

As of July 31, 2026, the Bank of Japan's guideline was to keep the uncollateralized overnight call rate at around **1.0%**. Monetary policy is decided by the BOJ Policy Board, not by the government. For investment purposes, I prefer to follow BOJ statements and MUFG's disclosed lending spreads rather than infer government intentions.

### 3. New NISA and asset management

I expected new-NISA inflows to create opportunities for Mitsubishi UFJ Asset Management and MUFG's trust, custody, and wealth businesses. The eMAXIS Slim fund family is one important customer touchpoint.

Assets under management do not translate one-for-one into group profit, however. Gross profit in MUFG's Asset Management and Investor Services business was ¥134.4 billion in Q1 FY2026, up ¥9.2 billion year over year. The asset-management component was ¥37.8 billion, up ¥1.4 billion. This is a real contributor, but it is not yet the group's main profit engine when compared with consolidated net income of ¥809.4 billion for the quarter.

:::comment
When I bought MUFG, my reasoning was that a weaker yen could benefit overseas earners, higher rates could improve lending income, and new NISA could support eMAXIS-related revenue.

The direction of the thesis is broadly unchanged, but the mechanics are more nuanced than I first assumed. MUFG shares are not dollars, and new-NISA inflows do not all become MUFG profit. Holding the company has made me break the original thesis into measurable parts.
:::

## What the latest results show

MUFG's Q1 FY2026 growth came from several sources, including higher rates, loan growth, fee income, and stronger equity-method earnings from Morgan Stanley.

| Metric | Q1 FY2025 | Q1 FY2026 | Year-on-year change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Net operating profit | ¥542.9B | ¥809.0B | +¥266.0B |
| Net income attributable to owners | ¥546.0B | ¥809.4B | +¥263.3B |
| Net interest income | ¥690.7B | ¥882.3B | +¥191.6B |
| AM/IS gross profit | ¥125.2B | ¥134.4B | +¥9.2B |

MUFG's consolidated CET1 ratio was 12.95% at June 30, 2026. Profit is not enough when assessing a bank; common equity available to absorb losses also matters. Overseas loans were ¥57.3 trillion, up ¥1.7 trillion from March, but the increase excluding currency effects was ¥1.0 trillion. This distinction separates underlying business growth from yen translation.

### Five items I check each quarter

1. Domestic loan rates, deposit rates, and lending spreads.
2. Loan balances and fee income.
3. Credit costs and the non-performing loan ratio.
4. Local-currency growth overseas versus currency translation.
5. The balance among the CET1 ratio, dividends, and buybacks.

Even when the policy rate rises, profit may not improve if deposit costs or credit losses rise faster. Conversely, loan growth and fee income can support earnings even if the policy rate remains unchanged.

## My conditions for adding

I use three broad conditions as a starting point:

- no material deterioration in business growth;
- domestic rates are stable or rising; and
- forward dividend yield, based on company guidance, exceeds 3%.

A 3% yield is not evidence of safety. It may simply reflect a falling share price or the market's expectation of a future dividend cut. Before adding, I check the earnings that fund the dividend, capital ratios, and management's shareholder-return policy.

:::comment
I review Japanese holdings after each result. I add when I have available capital and MUFG is more attractive than my other candidates.

For example, I have considered adding when capital rotated sharply into memory-related stocks and MUFG fell for reasons largely unrelated to its own operations. I try to distinguish business deterioration from market positioning and fund flows.
:::

## One rate cut does not automatically break the thesis

I originally viewed a turn from stable rates to rate cuts as the main invalidation condition. In a falling-rate environment, growth companies that benefit from cheaper financing can become relatively more attractive.

But one rate cut does not erase MUFG's value. Overseas earnings, fee businesses, and loan-volume growth can offset narrower domestic spreads. I now look for a combination of conditions:

- the BOJ enters a sustained easing cycle and domestic lending spreads contract;
- loan balances and fee income stall while operating profit declines over several quarters;
- an economic downturn pushes credit costs and non-performing loans materially higher;
- underlying overseas earnings weaken and yen translation becomes the main source of reported growth; or
- the CET1 ratio declines enough to weaken the sustainability of dividends and buybacks.

Capital is limited. Even if MUFG's earnings remain sound, falling rates may raise the expected return of other growth investments. I therefore treat holding an existing position and allocating new money as separate decisions.

## “A mega-bank cannot fail” is not an equity thesis

Large banks provide critical lending and payment functions, so their failure could destabilize the financial system. Japan has deposit insurance and an orderly resolution framework designed to preserve critical financial functions.

Those frameworks **do not guarantee shareholder value**. A bank's operations or insured deposits may be transferred or restructured while existing shareholders face dilution or severe losses. The FSA's explanation of bank resolution explicitly states that managers and shareholders of a failed institution should bear responsibility.

:::comment
I once thought that a mega-bank was effectively unable to fail because its collapse would damage Japan's financial system. I still believe authorities have strong incentives to keep critical financial functions operating.

But protecting the financial system and depositors is not the same as protecting shareholders. I no longer use systemic importance as a substitute for analyzing MUFG's earnings and capital.
:::

## I intend to hold for life, but not unconditionally

I bought MUFG with a minimum five-year horizon and, ideally, no planned sale date. Demand for financial infrastructure should persist, while its customer relationships, regulatory capabilities, and international network would be difficult to reproduce quickly.

Long-term ownership does not remove the need to review results. Each quarter, I look beyond policy rates to loans, fees, credit costs, and capital. A company can be important to society without producing an adequate return for shareholders.

## Summary

My MUFG thesis is broader than rising interest rates:

**Improving domestic lending spreads form the base, overseas and fee businesses diversify earnings, and adequate capital supports sustainable shareholder returns.**

Q1 FY2026 supported that thesis with substantial growth in net interest income and net profit. Lower rates, rising credit costs, and weakening overseas operations remain important risks. I will keep testing the position with multiple financial indicators rather than relying on the idea that a mega-bank is automatically safe.

This article is not a recommendation to buy or sell any security. Individual stocks can lose value or reduce dividends, and the criteria above are a record of my personal decision process.
