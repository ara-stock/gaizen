## AI Will Be Like Water or Electricity

When smartphones emerged, few immediately grasped that they would become the gateway to all information. AI is the same. In a few years, we will search, work, and make decisions through AI — and I'm convinced this shift is already underway.

:::comment
When Sam Altman said "AI will become like electricity," I was already thinking the exact same thing. People will pay a monthly AI bill just like a utility. And the companies that own that infrastructure will generate the most stable, durable long-term revenue. This conviction — that **AI will become the interface for everything** — is the foundation of every investment I'm about to describe.
:::

---

## Microsoft (MSFT) — Owning the Enterprise AI On-Ramp

### The OpenAI Stake + Cloud Flywheel

Microsoft has invested over $13 billion in OpenAI, securing preferential commercial rights to its models. Delivered via Azure OpenAI Service, when enterprises build with generative AI, most routes lead through Azure. This creates a structural loop: OpenAI improves → enterprises adopt via Azure → Azure revenue grows → Microsoft funds more AI R&D.

| Product | AI Integration |
|---------|---------------|
| Azure | AI API infrastructure, OpenAI partnership |
| Microsoft 365 | Copilot ($30/user/month add-on) |
| GitHub | Copilot for Developers (code completion) |
| LinkedIn | AI-driven hiring and talent matching |

:::comment
My reason for owning Microsoft is simple: **it holds a large stake in OpenAI**, so ChatGPT's growth directly benefits Microsoft. GitHub ties it to the developer ecosystem. Office 365 is already embedded in enterprises worldwide — layering AI (Copilot) on top of that existing base unlocks additional revenue from the same customers. As enterprise AI adoption accelerates, Microsoft is in the most advantageous position.

That said, there's a structural risk worth naming: **PowerPoint, Excel, Word — these tools may gradually become unnecessary as AI evolves.** If AI can generate presentations, build spreadsheets, and draft documents autonomously, the value of the tools themselves diminishes. This is a real threat to Microsoft's core Office revenue model. But that's precisely why Microsoft had no choice but to hold AI as a counter — the OpenAI investment is partly defensive. It's a bet on owning the thing that could displace you, before someone else does.
:::

### Financial Strength

- Revenue: $281.7B (FY2025 actual, +15% YoY)
- Azure revenue: $75B+ (FY2025, +34% YoY; +39% in Q4)
- Microsoft 365 Copilot enterprise adoption rapidly expanding
- Consistent dividend growth and buybacks

## Alphabet (GOOGL) — Self-Built AI with the World's Strongest Interface

### Gemini and Multimodal — Building AI In-House

Unlike Microsoft, Alphabet doesn't depend on an external partner for its core AI. **Gemini**, developed in-house, spans text, image, audio, and video — a multimodal capability that sets it apart. Delivered via Google Cloud for enterprise use and deeply integrated into Android and Chrome for consumers, Gemini's distribution reach is unmatched.

:::comment
The reason I hold Alphabet alongside Microsoft is precisely that **Alphabet builds its own AI**. Microsoft leans on OpenAI; Alphabet has Gemini. If AI models commoditize, owning the capability in-house beats sourcing it externally. Gemini's multimodal strength is a genuine differentiator — not just a brand claim.
:::

### Self-Designed TPUs — Independence from NVIDIA

Since 2015, Alphabet has designed its own Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) for AI training and inference. Running AI workloads without full dependence on NVIDIA reduces costs and preserves competitive flexibility. As AI infrastructure spend becomes a key battleground, this moat only grows more valuable.

### YouTube — The Interface That Won't Disappear

Even as AI reshapes how people find information, the human appetite for video content isn't going anywhere. YouTube's 2.5 billion monthly logged-in users make it the world's default video destination — across TV, smartphone, and PC.

:::comment
Even in an AI-first world, a frontend is always necessary. Alphabet holds both the frontend (Search, YouTube, Android) and the backend (Google Cloud, TPU) — all in-house. This is a structurally rare position. And as long as YouTube exists, advertising demand follows. The interface is the moat.
:::

| Asset | Strength |
|-------|---------|
| Gemini | In-house multimodal AI |
| Google Cloud | TPU + Gemini API infrastructure |
| Google Search | 90%+ global search share |
| Android | 72%+ global smartphone OS share |
| YouTube | 2.5B monthly users, video's default |
| Advertising | Dominant position in digital ads |

## Apple (AAPL) — A Major Access Point for AI

No matter how advanced AI becomes, users access it through a device. iPhone, Mac, AirPods, and Apple Watch form the most premium "AI frontend" in existence — and that's not changing.

:::comment
Even as AI interfaces evolve, smartphones and computers remain important access points. Apple connects iPhone, Mac, AirPods, and Apple Intelligence within its own ecosystem. I view that installed base and integration as competitive strengths worth monitoring.
:::

### Apple Intelligence and Privacy

Announced in 2024, Apple Intelligence combines on-device models with Private Cloud Compute. More complex requests can send necessary data to Apple's dedicated servers; Apple says that data is not stored and the privacy design can be independently verified. That approach may differentiate Apple in tightly regulated markets.

### Apple as a Counter to Microsoft

:::comment
**Apple is also a counter-position to Microsoft.** If PowerPoint and Excel become obsolete through AI, there's no longer a reason to stay on Windows. Leaving Office could translate directly into switching to Mac. AI development already favors Mac — engineers and creators know this. And the gravitational pull of iPhone + AirPods + Mac as a unified lifestyle is a brand force that logic alone can't explain.
:::

## Amazon (AMZN) — The Cloud Giant Backed by Anthropic

### AWS + Anthropic: The Other AI Alliance

In 2026, Amazon announced an additional $5 billion investment in Anthropic and plans for up to $20 billion more if specified commercial milestones are met. Claude is available through Amazon Bedrock and Claude Platform on AWS, deepening the relationship across both models and compute infrastructure.

:::comment
Amazon holding an Anthropic stake matters to me personally — and I'll note that this very site is built with Anthropic's Claude as its AI backbone. AWS already leads in cloud infrastructure. Adding Anthropic's AI capability on top creates a compelling enterprise AI platform. And Amazon also holds EC as a massive interface — one of the largest consumer touchpoints in the world. Amazon is one of the rare companies that simultaneously controls multiple interfaces *and* the infrastructure beneath them.
:::

### Revenue Structure

| Segment | Overview |
|---------|---------|
| AWS | Cloud infrastructure (generates the bulk of operating profit) |
| Advertising | Ads on the EC platform |
| E-commerce | Logistics, Prime subscription |
| Amazon Bedrock | Generative AI platform including Claude API |

AWS operating margins reached approximately 35% in FY2025 — AWS represents just 18% of total Amazon revenue but contributes roughly 60% of operating income, making it the engine that comfortably offsets the thinner margins of retail.

## One Framework: Device × Cloud × AI

:::comment
What these four companies share is that **each holds multiple touchpoints with users**. Microsoft: Office, GitHub, Azure. Alphabet: Search, YouTube, Android. Apple: iPhone, Mac, AirPods. Amazon: EC, AWS, Prime Video. None of them is a single-product company. And because of that, when AI proliferates, each is structurally positioned to capture the gains — across multiple entry points simultaneously.
:::

| Company | Device / Frontend | AI Model | Cloud Infrastructure |
|---------|------------------|---------|---------------------|
| Microsoft | Windows, Surface | GPT (OpenAI) | Azure |
| Alphabet | Android, Pixel | Gemini | Google Cloud |
| Apple | iPhone, Mac | Apple Intelligence | iCloud |
| Amazon | Alexa, Fire TV | Claude (Anthropic) | AWS |

Holding all four provides balanced exposure to "AI as infrastructure" — each with a distinct model, device, and cloud stack.

## Summary

Starting from the thesis that "AI will become infrastructure," I hold Microsoft, Alphabet, Apple, and Amazon as long-term core positions. Each controls a different interface and infrastructure stack — yet all four ride the same wave: AI embedding itself into every layer of daily life.

It looks like diversification. But it's actually concentrated exposure to a single scenario: AI becoming the interface for everything.
