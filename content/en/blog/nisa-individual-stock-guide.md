## What the Growth Investment Quota Allows

Japan's NISA Growth Investment Quota (成長投資枠) allows up to ¥2.4M per year (¥12M lifetime) in tax-exempt individual stock holdings — including US equities like Apple, NVIDIA, and Amazon.

**The basic flow:**

1. Activate the Growth Investment Quota in your NISA account
2. Look up the ticker symbol for your target stock
3. Choose how to handle currency (JPY-denominated settlement or convert to USD yourself)
4. Place an order (market or limit)
5. Confirm the holding shows up under your NISA Growth Quota

## Which Brokerage to Use

Both Rakuten Securities and SBI Securities support US stocks in the Growth Investment Quota.

**Rakuten Securities** offers yen-denominated settlement (円貨決済) — you order in yen without manually converting to dollars first. Lower friction, no need to time the exchange rate. Good starting point.

**SBI Securities** has a foreign currency accumulation feature — you can gradually accumulate USD daily, then use that to buy US stocks. Useful for spreading FX cost over time.

Either works. If you already have a primary account, start there.

## How to Think About Stock Selection

Unlike index funds, individual stocks require company-specific research. The returns are less predictable and position sizing matters more.

### Entry-level stocks worth understanding

**Apple (AAPL)**
Shifted from hardware-dependent (iPhone, Mac) to services (App Store, iCloud, Apple Pay) — a more recurring, higher-margin revenue mix. Ecosystem lock-in and brand strength are difficult to replicate.

**Microsoft (MSFT)**
Office + Azure cloud + Copilot AI + LinkedIn creates diversified revenue that holds up in downturns. Enterprise SaaS contracts provide recurring income stability.

**NVIDIA (NVDA)**
Dominant market share in AI datacenter GPUs. Growth is exceptional, but valuation (PER) is correspondingly high. Short-term volatility is significant — position accordingly.

**Amazon (AMZN)**
E-commerce and AWS cloud are the two pillars. AWS margin subsidizes the retail business and drives long-term value. AWS growth is what matters for the stock.

These are all top S&P 500 holdings. The investment thesis here is: use index funds for broad market exposure, then deliberately overweight specific companies where you have higher conviction.

## Handling Currency Risk

US stocks are denominated in dollars. Yen appreciation reduces the yen-equivalent value of your holdings, even if the stock itself rises.

**How to think about it:**

Over long time horizons — 10+ years — company earnings growth dominates currency fluctuation. A business that grows earnings 15% annually for a decade will overwhelm most realistic FX scenarios.

Rather than waiting for a favorable exchange rate (which is market timing by another name), buying in increments over time is more practical. The same logic that applies to stock price timing applies here.

## Order Types: Market vs. Limit

**Market order**: Executes immediately at the current price. You get the trade; you don't control the price.

**Limit order**: You specify a maximum price. Executes only if the stock reaches your price. You control the entry; you might not get filled.

US markets trade during Japan nighttime (22:30–5:00 JST, one hour earlier during US daylight saving time). If you can't monitor the market in real time, a limit order prevents you from getting filled at a bad price during volatile overnight sessions.

## Tax Treatment in NISA: What's Covered and What Isn't

**Capital gains (price appreciation):** Fully tax-exempt in NISA. Buying a stock at ¥100,000 and selling at ¥200,000 produces zero taxable income. This is where NISA's Growth Quota is most powerful for individual stocks.

**Dividends:** Tax-exempt domestically, but **US withholding tax (10%) is still applied by the IRS before dividends reach your account.** NISA doesn't help with foreign withholding. If dividend income is a primary goal, this 10% drag matters.

The implication: **NISA Growth Quota is most tax-efficient for growth stocks where the return comes from price appreciation, not dividends.**

## Quota Management

The Growth Quota is ¥2.4M per year, ¥12M lifetime. When you sell, the used quota doesn't immediately restore — it restores the following year (up to annual limits).

Frequent buying and selling burns through quota faster and reduces how much you can hold tax-exempt long-term. This reinforces a buy-and-hold approach. Using NISA for active trading is wasteful.

## Summary

- Growth Investment Quota: ¥2.4M/year, ¥12M lifetime, for individual stocks and ETFs
- Rakuten Securities' yen settlement is the easiest entry point; SBI's FX accumulation helps spread currency cost
- Entry-level US stocks: Apple, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Amazon — all top S&P 500 holdings
- Capital gains are fully tax-exempt; US dividend withholding (10%) is not recoverable
- Use the quota for long-term positions, not active trading
- The most practical structure: S&P 500 index fund as the foundation, individual stocks as deliberate overweights where you have conviction
