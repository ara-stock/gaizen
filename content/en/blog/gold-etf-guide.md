## The Short Answer: GLDM for Smaller Amounts, GLD for Larger Ones

The framework for picking a gold ETF is simple: **choose the lowest expense ratio, then verify liquidity matches your position size.**

For investments in the range of a few thousand to a few hundred thousand yen (roughly $100–$5,000), **GLDM (SPDR Gold MiniShares)** is the straightforward choice. For larger transactions, **GLD (SPDR Gold Shares)** offers deeper liquidity that becomes meaningful at scale.

---

## Why Expense Ratio Is the Primary Factor

Gold ETFs generate no dividends and no interest. Over a long holding period, **cost differences compound directly into return differences.**

| | GLDM | GLD |
|---|---|---|
| Annual expense ratio | **0.10%** | 0.40% |
| AUM | ~$14 billion | ~$100 billion |
| Average daily volume | ~6.7M shares | ~12.6M shares |
| 5-year CAGR | ~19.7% | ~19.4% |

GLDM costs a quarter of what GLD charges, and its five-year CAGR edges out GLD's — the cost advantage showing up directly in returns.

## Checking Liquidity Before You Buy

The second factor is **liquidity — how deep the order book is.**

GLDM's ~6.7M daily share volume is more than sufficient for individual investors transacting at typical retail sizes. But when position sizes grow, it's worth checking whether your order can be filled at a reasonable price without moving the market.

Before placing a large order:

1. Pull up the live order book
2. Confirm there's sufficient volume at your target price range
3. Check that your expected fill price won't deviate significantly from the quoted price

For large-scale transactions, GLD's ~$100B AUM and ~12.6M daily shares provide institutional-grade depth. That's the cushion that matters when moving meaningful size.

## Japan-Specific Consideration: Gold ETF vs. Physical Gold Accumulation

For Japanese residents, gold ETFs and physical gold savings plans (純金積立) are taxed differently.

| | Gold ETF | Physical gold / accumulation plan |
|---|---|---|
| Tax treatment | Separate taxation (flat 20.315%) | Aggregate income tax (capital gains) |
| Long-term benefit (5+ years) | None | **Taxable gain reduced by half** |
| Special deduction | None | **¥500,000 deduction available** |
| Loss carryforward | Can offset stock/ETF gains | Cannot offset |

If your total aggregate income (salary + other) stays below ¥9 million, a long-held physical gold position can result in an effective tax rate below 16.5% — lower than the ETF's flat 20.315%.

That said, physical gold plans carry purchase spreads and storage costs. **ETFs win on cost efficiency and convenience; physical gold can win on tax treatment** — which is better depends on your holding period and income level.

## Risks to Understand Before Buying

Gold is often described as a safe-haven asset, but that does not mean its price is stable.

1. **It produces no dividends or interest**: returns depend on price appreciation
2. **It can weaken when interest rates rise**: income-producing assets may become more attractive
3. **JPY returns include currency risk**: a stronger yen can reduce returns for Japanese investors
4. **It can fall during crises**: investors may sell gold when they urgently need liquidity

Rather than trying to predict short-term prices, define why gold belongs in the portfolio and set a maximum allocation in advance.

:::comment
I moved from a gold ETF to a physical gold accumulation plan, mainly for the tax treatment. For a long-term holder with my income level, the physical route made more sense on an after-tax basis.

When I was using an ETF, it was GLDM. Low cost, easy to access, no complexity — exactly what individual investors need.

I once assumed finite supply made gold relatively resistant to declines. In practice, gold can still fall sharply because of interest rates, currency movements, and investor liquidity needs. I now focus more on managing its portfolio allocation than predicting its next move.
