## BTC Is a Commodity, Not a Currency

The word "cryptocurrency" implies currency. But I think of BTC as closer to a **commodity** — alongside gold, silver, crude oil, corn, art, and wine.

What connects these assets? Their value doesn't derive from a clear, measurable utility. It exists because a sufficient number of people agree it exists. Gold doesn't pay dividends. A painting doesn't generate cash flow. BTC doesn't power anything. Yet all of them hold value because markets of believers sustain that value.

BTC is a digital commodity. That framing matters for how I hold it.

:::comment
Debating whether BTC works as a "currency" misses the point, in my view. Gold hasn't been used as cash in a century — but its value is uncontested. BTC fits the same mental model: scarcity-backed, market-sustained value. Once I stopped thinking of it as a currency and started thinking of it as a commodity, my position on how to hold it became much clearer.
:::

## How BTC Is Designed to Hold Value

### Mining: Reward for Solving Cryptography

BTC is issued through mining. Computers on the network compete to solve complex cryptographic puzzles. The first to solve it receives BTC as a reward. This is the origin of every BTC in existence.

### The Supply Cap and the Halving

BTC has a **hard cap of 21 million coins**. And roughly every four years, a "halving" event cuts the mining reward in half.

| Halving | Year | Block Reward |
|---------|------|-------------|
| 1st | 2012 | 25 BTC |
| 2nd | 2016 | 12.5 BTC |
| 3rd | 2020 | 6.25 BTC |
| 4th | 2024 | 3.125 BTC |
| 5th (next) | **2028** | 1.5625 BTC |

:::comment
The halving is elegant because it's automatic. No human decides when to tighten supply. The protocol does it mechanically, on a schedule that everyone can see. Each cycle, scarcity increases by design. That systematic quality is one of BTC's genuine strengths — and one of the reasons it's still standing when most early crypto projects have collapsed.
:::

## How I View BTC at Its Current Scale

BTC's market capitalization changes with its price every day, but it is already large enough to be compared with major listed companies. I do not assume that an asset at this scale will repeat the percentage gains of its earliest years. Position size needs to reflect both upside and drawdown risk.

:::comment
BTC is no longer a small corner of the crypto market. As market capitalization grows, repeating the same percentage gain requires progressively more capital. Because future prices cannot be predicted reliably, I manage the risk through position size rather than a price target.
:::

## Lost Keys Are a Custody Risk, Not an Investment Benefit

BTC can become inaccessible when the owner loses access to the private key.

- A wallet's private key is forgotten or destroyed
- BTC is sent to the wrong address (called "GOX" in the crypto community — derived from the infamous Mt. Gox exchange collapse)
- An exchange is hacked

Inaccessible coins may stop moving on-chain, but their exact amount cannot be verified. I do not count accidental loss as part of the investment case. It is a custody risk that needs to be managed through exchange selection, wallet practices, and backups.

:::comment
Bitcoin's issuance cap and an owner losing a private key are separate issues. I treat the latter as a risk of self-custody and third-party custody, not as evidence that the asset should become more valuable.
:::

## My Position: BTC as a Hedge, Not a Core Holding

Let me be honest about where BTC sits in my portfolio.

**Equities are the primary asset.** Owning businesses — through index funds or individual stocks — is the foundation of wealth building. BTC plays a supporting role:

- Inflation hedge
- Optionality for unexpected price appreciation
- Portfolio diversification element

:::comment
The return-to-risk ratio for BTC does not justify a large position for me. I hold it because not holding it feels like leaving optionality on the table, but I am not counting on a particular price outcome. Equities come first. Business ownership creates real value; BTC remains dependent on continued market demand.
:::

## Altcoins and the Competitive Landscape

More tokens with equity-like mechanics are emerging, competing with BTC's store-of-value narrative. Meanwhile, altcoins and meme coins will likely continue shrinking — the vast majority won't survive the next cycle.

BTC has structural advantages — a fixed supply cap, proven network effects, over a decade of survival. But I can't say with certainty that BTC will survive long-term.

A meaningful shift is underway: on-chain tokens that behave like equities are now available, meaning you no longer need BTC specifically to hold on-chain value. The equation of "owning crypto = owning BTC" may no longer hold in the years ahead.

:::comment
I think BTC probably survives — but I won't claim certainty. The emergence of stock-equivalent tokens on-chain is a different kind of competition than altcoins or meme coins. It raises a genuine question: if you can hold productive, equity-like assets on-chain, why hold BTC at all? I don't have a definitive answer. I'll keep watching and adjust the position as the landscape becomes clearer.
:::
