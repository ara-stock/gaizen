## What Is Index Investing?

Index investing aims to match the performance of a specific benchmark index rather than selecting individual companies. Instead of researching and picking stocks, you buy the index — i.e., the market itself.

Both the All-Country global equity index and the S&P 500 can work for long-term accumulation. I initially built my portfolio around the S&P 500. Today, individual stocks carry more weight and I judge them primarily on business growth and durability, but I still treat the S&P 500 as one long-term holding. All-Country remains a rational alternative for investors who prefer broader geographic diversification.

## The S&P500 Index

The S&P500 covers approximately 500 companies listed on major US exchanges. It's not simply the 500 largest — only companies meeting **strict criteria for market cap, liquidity, and profitability** are included. The current count is around 503 stocks.

Weights are determined by **market-cap weighting** — companies with larger market capitalizations occupy larger portions of the index.

### Why Market-Cap Weighting Aligns with My Investment Principles

Apple, Microsoft, and NVIDIA hold the top positions for a reason. Market cap is large because brand, strategy, and investor confidence are all present. The index automatically reflects that.

> A company is large because of a reason. Brand, strategy, and investor conviction — all three must be present before market cap grows.

Market-cap weighting aggregates the collective judgment of all market participants and automatically applies it to your portfolio. Strong companies gain weight; declining companies lose weight — not by rule, but by market dynamics.

## The Goal: Inflation Hedge and Long-Term Growth

### Equities as an Inflation Hedge

Inflation means more money chasing the same goods — the purchasing power of cash erodes over time even when the nominal balance doesn't change.

Equities benefit from inflation through **price-passing**: when input costs rise, businesses raise prices for products and services. Nominal revenue and earnings rise, and stock prices follow. Holding equities is a long-term mechanism for protecting assets from fiat currency dilution.

## Risks: An Honest Accounting

### US Concentration Risk

S&P500 is 500 US companies only. The current global economy is US-centric — tech, finance, and consumer goods are led by US companies. But that's not guaranteed to persist.

China is growing in rare earths, manufacturing, and AI. Europe holds irreplaceable positions in semiconductor equipment (ASML and others). Global economic leadership is shifting, gradually.

S&P500 outperforms while US dominance continues — but if geopolitical rebalancing accelerates, All-Country's geographic diversification becomes more valuable. The choice comes down to your view on how long US structural advantage persists.

### Other Risks

- **Currency risk** — In a strong-yen environment, JPY-denominated returns compress
- **Valuation risk** — S&P500's P/E is historically elevated; some degree of overvaluation is hard to deny
- **Short-term drawdowns are inevitable** — 5–20% corrections happen regularly. Without long-term conviction, the benefits of dollar-cost averaging are difficult to capture

## Summary

Accumulating eMAXIS Slim S&P 500 provides broad exposure to major US companies while keeping the process simple. It can support long-term growth and offer some protection against inflation, but neither outcome is guaranteed.

The S&P 500 is no longer the foundation of my investment decisions. I hold it alongside individual companies that I research directly. Its role is to provide a long-term, low-maintenance allocation while I accept and monitor the concentration risk in US equities.
