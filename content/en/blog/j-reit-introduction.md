## Thinking of J-REITs as "High-Yield Products" Misses the Point

J-REITs (Japanese Real Estate Investment Trusts) are often described as high-yield instruments — distribution yields in the 4–5% range are common. But that framing misses the more important insight.

**The core strength of J-REITs is that they hold their value when equity markets are falling.**

When markets crash, people don't stop living in rental apartments. Companies don't immediately vacate their offices (at least before COVID, that held almost universally). Real-world demand for physical space doesn't evaporate overnight the way stock prices can.

I bought J-REITs shortly after the COVID crash. Then, as the bull market took hold, I rotated into equities. My reasoning: **in a phase where businesses are growing fast, owning businesses beats owning land.** That framing — not yield-chasing — is what guides how I use J-REITs.

This article covers the structure of J-REITs and how I think about when to hold them versus equities.

---

## What Is a J-REIT?

A J-REIT (Japan Real Estate Investment Trust) is a listed investment vehicle that pools capital from investors to buy and operate real estate, then distributes rental income and sale proceeds to unitholders. The market launched on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in 2001.

As of March 2026, **58 J-REITs are listed on the TSE**, with a combined market capitalization of approximately **¥14.6 trillion** (source: Tokyo Stock Exchange). That makes Japan the world's third-largest REIT market.

J-REITs trade like stocks through any securities account. Entry points range from a few tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand yen depending on the individual REIT.

---

## Why J-REITs Distribute 90%+ of Profits

The reason J-REITs can maintain high distribution yields comes down to a specific tax rule.

Under Japan's Special Taxation Measures Act, J-REITs that distribute **more than 90% of distributable profits** to investors are treated as conduit entities — meaning the REIT itself pays little to no corporate income tax. Ordinary corporations pay roughly 30% corporate tax; a qualifying J-REIT avoids most of that.

In exchange, J-REITs cannot retain meaningful earnings. Most J-REITs distribute close to **100% of profits**. Capital for growth comes from new borrowing or additional unit issuance — not internal reserves.

| | Ordinary Corporation (Stocks) | J-REIT |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate Tax | ~30% | Near zero if conditions met |
| Distribution Ratio | Discretionary (can retain earnings) | 90%+ effectively required |
| Growth Funding | Retained earnings / borrowing / equity | Borrowing / unit issuance |

## J-REIT Sectors

J-REITs are categorized by the type of property they hold.

| Sector | Overview | Characteristics |
|---|---|---|
| **Office** | Commercial buildings in urban centers | Largest sector (50%+). Cyclical; occupancy rates fluctuate with the economy |
| **Residential** | Apartments and condominiums | Stable as long as population exists. Low economic sensitivity |
| **Logistics** | Warehouses and distribution centers | Growing with e-commerce. Long-term leases provide stable income |
| **Retail** | Shopping malls and commercial properties | Sensitive to consumer trends and external shocks like COVID |
| **Hotel** | Business hotels and ryokan | Tied to inbound tourism. High variability |
| **Diversified** | Mix of multiple sectors | Built-in diversification |

Office and residential make up the bulk of the market. Logistics REITs have grown in prominence as e-commerce expands. Senior care facilities are also emerging as a sector, driven by Japan's aging demographic.

## Real Estate's Edge Is in the Downturn

This is the core idea.

When equity markets crash — from earnings fears, financial contagion, geopolitical risk — stock prices can fall violently. But during that same period, **people keep renting apartments, and companies keep paying for office space.**

Because real-world demand underpins the cash flows, J-REITs tend to hold their value better than stocks in severe downturns, and they become relatively cheap when equities are in free fall.

:::comment
When COVID hit and equities collapsed, I turned to J-REITs. My reasoning was straightforward: no matter how bad the market gets, people need somewhere to live and companies need somewhere to operate. While J-REIT prices had dropped sharply, the actual rental income from those properties wasn't going to disappear overnight. That disconnect between price and underlying cash flow was the opportunity.
:::

The caveat: this logic has limits. COVID itself demonstrated that — office REITs and hotel REITs suffered badly as remote work spread and travel stopped. **The type of real estate matters enormously.** Residential and logistics held up far better than hotel or retail.

## In a Bull Market, Equities Win

The reason I sold J-REITs when the market turned up: **businesses grow; land doesn't grow at the same rate.**

Real estate value is constrained by supply and demand for physical space. Growth is possible, but gradual. Businesses, by contrast, can grow through innovation, new markets, and acquisition — sometimes exponentially. When the economy is expanding and businesses are compounding value, **equities capture more of that upside than land does.**

| Phase | Favored Asset | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Market crash / high uncertainty | J-REIT (residential, logistics) | Real-world demand provides a floor; prices can become compelling |
| Bull market / economic expansion | Equities | Business growth drives returns far above land appreciation |
| Rising interest rates | J-REITs face headwinds | Higher borrowing costs reduce profitability; yield comparison shifts |
| Rate cuts / monetary easing | Tailwind for J-REITs | Lower funding costs; yield attractiveness increases |

There is also a specific short-term angle I occasionally revisit: **major new property developments**. When a large building or logistics center is announced and scheduled for completion, the expected boost to rental income can be a catalyst worth monitoring.

## How to Choose: Three Criteria

Rather than memorizing the distribution schedule of every J-REIT, filtering by three objective criteria makes selection manageable.

**① Large Market Capitalization**

Large J-REITs hold diversified property portfolios, so a single vacancy or disposal has limited impact on overall income. Liquidity is also higher, making it easier to buy and sell. Starting from the **top 10 J-REITs by market cap** is a sound first filter.

Major large-cap J-REITs as of 2026 (approximate order by market cap):

| Ticker | Name | Sector | Distribution Months (Approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8951 | Nippon Building Fund | Office | Mar / Sep |
| 8952 | Japan Real Estate Investment | Office | Mar / Sep |
| 3462 | Nomura Real Estate Master Fund | Diversified | Feb / Aug |
| 3283 | Nippon Prologis REIT | Logistics | Feb / Aug |
| 8984 | Daiwa House REIT | Diversified | Mar / Sep |
| 3269 | Advance Residence Investment | Residential | Apr / Oct |
| 3281 | GLP J-REIT | Logistics | May / Nov |

Fiscal periods can change — confirm on each REIT's IR page before purchasing. By combining REITs with different distribution months, you can build a portfolio that delivers income every month.

**② Low Management Expense Ratio**

J-REITs incur asset management fees, custodian fees, and administrative fees. The combined expense ratio — measured against net assets — is the relevant cost figure. Most J-REITs fall in the **0.5–1.5% range**; lower means more cash flows through to unitholders.

Where to check: each REIT's **financial statements (決算短信)** or **management report (運用報告書)**, or the expense section on [JAPAN-REIT.COM](https://www.japan-reit.com/) individual REIT pages.

**③ High Sharpe Ratio**

The Sharpe ratio divides return by risk (standard deviation) — a useful filter for identifying REITs that deliver reasonable returns with lower price volatility.

That said, **individual J-REIT Sharpe ratios are not publicly available through most free sources**. Professional platforms like Bloomberg or premium brokerage research tools carry this data, but it is not easily accessible for individual investors.

A practical substitute: after filtering by market cap and expense ratio, **review each REIT's price chart to assess the depth and recovery speed of past drawdowns**. A REIT that fell less and recovered faster during past market stress is a reasonable proxy for a favorable risk-adjusted profile.

## Tax Treatment

Distributions from J-REITs are subject to **withholding tax at approximately 20.315%** — the same flat rate as stock dividends under Japan's separate self-assessed taxation system. Holding J-REITs in a NISA account makes them tax-free.

One important distinction from ordinary stock dividends: **J-REIT distributions are not eligible for the dividend tax credit (配当控除)**. Stock investors who choose comprehensive income taxation can claim a credit on dividend income; J-REIT holders cannot.

| | Stock Dividends | J-REIT Distributions |
|---|---|---|
| Tax Rate (Flat) | ~20.315% | ~20.315% |
| Dividend Tax Credit | Available (if comprehensive taxation chosen) | Not available |
| NISA | Tax-free | Tax-free |

## Who Should Consider J-REITs?

My honest view: J-REITs are not a permanent holding for me — they are a situational tool.

**J-REITs make sense when:**
- Equity markets have fallen sharply and J-REIT prices look genuinely cheap relative to underlying cash flows
- You have reached FIRE or retirement and want stable distributions without the mental overhead of monitoring individual stock positions
- You want your portfolio to have partial exposure to the characteristics of physical real estate

**Equities should take priority when:**
- The economy is expanding and business growth is compounding
- You are still in the wealth-building phase and want to maximize compound returns

:::comment
Even after reaching FIRE, checking stock prices every day can become a psychological burden. That's a phase where combining monthly-distribution J-REITs and investment trusts to create a passive cash flow structure might make sense to me. But I want to be clear: that's a drawdown strategy, not a wealth-building strategy. In the accumulation phase, equities are the better tool.
:::

---

## Summary

- **J-REITs' core advantage is stability in downturns** — rental income is underpinned by real-world demand that doesn't disappear when stock prices fall
- **In bull markets, equities are superior** — businesses compound value faster than land in a growth phase
- **The 90%+ distribution rule** produces high yields but prevents meaningful retained earnings; growth requires ongoing capital raises
- **Staggering fiscal periods** across multiple J-REITs allows for monthly distribution income
- **In the FIRE / post-retirement phase**, J-REITs are a practical tool for generating steady cash flow with reduced monitoring
